## Inventory and evidence

Personal information includes business email addresses, identity keys, device/user associations, free-text answers about staff, and linkable audit IDs. Assessment data can also be commercially sensitive even when it is not personal information. Hashing an identifier does not necessarily anonymise it.

Access shorthand: **C** = authenticated customer with the relevant subject, tenant, organisation and membership/entitlement checks; **O** = authorised operator with the specific capability; **S** = service identity/database runtime; **P** = configured provider and its authorised personnel under arrangements still to be verified. Cloud/database administrators may have wider access than the app's roles; no actual roster was verified. Consultant access must remain limited to assigned client organisations, not all tenants.

## NZ information privacy principles: readiness assessment

The [OPC principles](https://www.privacy.org.nz/privacy-principles/) govern personal information, not just consumer accounts. This is an evidence/gap map, not a finding that every listed gap is a proven breach. IPP 3A has applied since 1 May 2026. Statutory exceptions require case-specific legal assessment.

| Principle | Evidence / unresolved readiness action |
| --- | --- |
| 1 — necessary collection | Scope and assessment schemas exist. Document a lawful service purpose for each field; minimise identifying free text and avoid collecting secrets or unnecessary staff details. |
| 2 — source | Direct signup is distinguishable from consultant/client/staff information. Establish the lawful basis for indirect collection; organisation authority alone is not universal individual authorisation. |
| 3 — direct notice | Existing privacy page provides some information. Replace unsupported deployment/retention assurances; identify collecting/holding agency and address, recipients, required/optional fields and consequences at collection. |
| [3A — indirect notice](https://www.privacy.org.nz/privacy-principles/3a/) | Consultant submissions and invitations can concern other people. Decide agency/agent roles, notice delivery as soon as reasonably practicable, evidence of delivery, and documented exceptions; do not shift all responsibility to the consultant. |
| 4 — fair collection | Authentication and explicit workflow inputs are controls. Add proportionate identity verification and clear collection prompts; avoid demanding identity documents by ordinary email as a default. |
| [5 — security](https://www.privacy.org.nz/privacy-principles/5/) | Tenant/subject checks, RLS, capability gates and no-store responses exist. Verify deployed configuration, privileged staff access, incident response, provider safeguards and restoration handling. Source controls alone do not establish operational compliance. |
| [6 — access](https://www.privacy.org.nz/privacy-principles/6/) | Lifecycle organisation JSON export exists but is incomplete for personal-information access. Provide a staffed request path now; verify requester, search all relevant stores and review others' information before disclosure. |
| [7 — correction](https://www.privacy.org.nz/privacy-principles/7/) | Some records are editable and operator corrections exist; immutable evidence needs linked correction/statements. Establish refusal reasons, attached statements and reasonable recipient notification where required. |
| [8 — accuracy](https://www.privacy.org.nz/privacy-principles/8/) | Revisioned answers/provenance assist review. Check currency and distinguish user statements from generated inference before using or sharing results. |
| [9 — retention](https://www.privacy.org.nz/privacy-principles/9/) | Several schedules exist but do not cover every store or enforce expiry. Approve purpose-based periods and disposal verification, including backups and legal holds; no TTL is not permission to retain indefinitely. |
| [10 — use](https://www.privacy.org.nz/privacy-principles/10/) | Separate service delivery, security and AI purposes. Do not silently reuse assessments for marketing/model development; assess any secondary use and its lawful basis. |
| [11 — disclosure](https://www.privacy.org.nz/privacy-principles/11/) | Tenant restrictions help. Specify consultants, staff and providers who receive data; verify scope of exports and each external integration; document exceptions rather than offering unrestricted disclosure rights. |
| [12 — overseas](https://www.privacy.org.nz/privacy-principles/12/) | Verify regions, subprocessors and contracts. Determine whether each provider acts solely as an agent or receives a disclosure for its own purposes; document the applicable safeguards/basis. No blanket overseas-consent checkbox substitutes for this analysis. |
| 13 — identifiers | Subject, tenant and organisation keys exist. Limit identifier use to necessary linkage, protect mappings and verify identity before assignment/disclosure; do not solicit government identifiers for convenience. |

Also appoint a [privacy officer](https://www.privacy.org.nz/responsibilities/your-obligations/); establish a [breach response](https://www.privacy.org.nz/responsibilities/privacy-breaches/) that assesses serious harm and notifies OPC/affected people as soon as practicable when required. Do not misstate a recommended 72-hour target as the Act's universal statutory deadline.
