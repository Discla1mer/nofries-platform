Your top five gaps

How the gaps are ranked

The check combines each question’s weight with how far the answer is from fully in place. “I don’t know” also counts as a gap. This order guides planning; it is not an official NCSC priority.

  1. Data Recovery: backups

    Answer: No

    Why it matters: If ransomware, theft or a fault wipes your files, a backup the attacker can't reach is how you get the business running again.

    What to do next: Add one offline copy, or one that cannot be changed or deleted.

  2. Data Recovery: restore tests

    Answer: Sometimes, but not written down or checked

    Why it matters: A backup that has never been restored may not work when you need it. Testing shows how long recovery really takes.

    What to do next: Restore one real file now, and put a fuller test in the diary.

  3. Patching

    Answer: No

    Why it matters: Attackers use known flaws in out-of-date software to break in. Updates close those flaws.

    What to do next: Turn on automatic updates. If you have a website, ask your host or developer to confirm it is patched.

  4. Limit supplier access

    Answer: I don't know

    Why it matters: A supplier's stolen login can become a way into your data. Limits and an extra login step reduce what an attacker can reach.

    What to do next: List who has access, turn on the extra login step, and add a breach-notice clause.

  5. Multi-factor Authentication: coverage

    Answer: Partly in place

    Why it matters: A stolen or guessed password is enough to take over an account unless a second login step stands in the way.

    What to do next: Turn the extra login step on for these services, then review any exceptions.

Example questionnaire answers

Four examples from the same fictional answers. Check the coverage before using them on an insurer or customer form.

  1. Is an extra login step (MFA) required for email, remote access, admin accounts, finance tools and other important online services?

    Suggested answer: Partly

    Your saved answer: Partly in place.

    To answer Yes: Turn the extra login step on for these services, then review any exceptions.

  2. Are your important files and systems backed up automatically, with at least three copies on two kinds of storage, including one copy offsite and one copy offline or otherwise out of reach of ransomware?

    Suggested answer: No

    Your saved answer: No.

    To answer Yes: Add one offline copy, or one that cannot be changed or deleted.

  3. Do people only get the access they need for their job, with a separate admin login from everyday email, and do you remove or review access when someone joins, changes role or leaves?

    Suggested answer: Partly

    Your saved answer: Partly in place.

    To answer Yes: Separate admin logins from everyday ones, and check access every three months, including people who have left.

  4. If someone asks you to change bank or invoice details, reset a password or change a sensitive login, do you check the request by a second method, such as a call to a known number, rather than replying to the same message?

    Suggested answer: Yes

    Your saved answer: Yes, in place and checked.

    To answer Yes: Require a check by a second method, and never approve from the original message alone.
